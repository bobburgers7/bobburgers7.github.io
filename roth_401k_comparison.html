▶ 📖 How to Use This Calculator

This calculator helps you optimize your 401k contribution strategy by comparing two scenarios:

  • Scenario A: Maximize Roth 401k contributions ($24,500 in 2026), remainder to Traditional
  • Scenario B: All contributions to Traditional 401k

📝 Step-by-Step Guide:

  1. Enter your current situation: Age, income, existing 401k balances, and annual contribution budget
  2. Configure retirement details: Retirement age, working/retirement states, Social Security benefits
  3. Choose conversion strategy: Select how aggressively you want to convert Traditional → Roth during retirement (ages 59-74)
  4. Review the recommendation: The calculator suggests an optimal contribution strategy based on your inputs
  5. Explore the breakdown: Click the collapsed sections to see detailed year-by-year tax calculations

Key Features:

  • Comprehensive tax modeling: Includes federal/state taxes, IRMAA surcharges, and Social Security taxation
  • Roth conversion optimization: Automatically fills up to your target tax bracket during retirement
  • RMD impact analysis: Shows the effect of Required Minimum Distributions starting at age 75
  • State tax arbitrage: Compares working vs retirement state taxes

Important: This is a planning tool, not financial advice. Tax laws change, and individual situations vary. Consider consulting a tax professional or financial advisor for personalized guidance.

▶ 🔬 Research Insights: Understanding Roth vs Traditional Decisions

This calculator synthesizes insights from two complementary research approaches: McQuarrie's full lifecycle analysis and Vanguard's Break-Even Tax Rate (BETR) methodology.

📊 McQuarrie's Full Lifecycle Analysis

Models every year from now through age 90, capturing RMDs, Social Security taxation, IRMAA surcharges, and the "supersaver problem" where large Traditional balances force high-bracket withdrawals.

📈 Vanguard's BETR Method

Calculates the "break-even" future tax rate where Roth and Traditional are equivalent. Key insight: how you invest tax savings matters—tax drag can make Roth beneficial even when future rates are lower.

⚠️ When These Methods Disagree

BETR uses a single future rate (your expected retirement marginal rate), while McQuarrie's method captures rate changes over time. For "supersavers" with large Traditional balances, RMDs will push you into higher brackets than BETR assumes—causing the methods to disagree. In such cases, trust the full calculation as it models your actual tax trajectory.

📖 What Conventional Wisdom Says:

  1. Convert if tax rate is lower now than future: Intertemporal tax arbitrage
  2. Don't convert if tax rate is higher now: Unless tax rates will rise
  3. Indifferent if rates are the same: After-tax wealth should be identical

The Problem: This oversimplifies. McQuarrie shows paying conversion tax from outside funds isn't as beneficial as believed. Vanguard shows that tax drag on invested savings changes the math entirely.

💡 Key Insights from Vanguard's BETR Research (July 2025):

  • How tax savings are invested matters enormously: If you invest Traditional tax savings in tax-inefficient assets (REITs, bonds), the tax drag can make Roth beneficial even if future rates drop significantly
  • BETR < Current Rate is possible: Due to tax drag, the break-even rate can be lower than your current marginal rate
  • Roth-equivalent reinvestment is rare: Unless tax savings go into a Roth IRA, they face some form of taxation that erodes growth

🔍 Mike Piper's Critique of BETR (Oblivious Investor)

While endorsing BETR's core insights, Piper notes important limitations of using BETR alone:

  • Cash assumption is unrealistic: BETR's "pay with cash" scenario shows very low break-even rates, but assumes the cash sits idle for 20+ years. In reality, that cash might be spent on bills soon—meaning you'd liquidate appreciated assets instead, triggering capital gains.
  • Capital gains tax ignored: BETR assumes no capital gains tax when liquidating taxable assets to pay conversion tax. In practice, highly appreciated assets might be better preserved for step-up in basis at death.
  • Full lifecycle planning is preferable: Rather than calculating a break-even rate, Piper recommends modeling projected cash flows through retirement—exactly what this calculator does.

Bottom Line: "I don't think this is the best way to do a Roth conversion analysis. Rather, I think it's preferable to look at Roth conversions as part of a much broader analysis, modeling projected cash flows through retirement." — Mike Piper, CPA

📊 How Much Traditional Balance Triggers High Brackets?

At age 75 (2026 dollars, inflation-adjusted):
• 22% bracket: ~$2M+ Traditional (RMD ≈ $81k)
• 24% bracket: ~$3.5M+ Traditional
• 32% bracket: ~$8M+ Traditional

This is the "supersaver problem"—large balances force high-bracket RMDs regardless of your earlier retirement income.

🔍 Key Findings That Challenge Conventional Wisdom:

  • Future tax rates need not be higher for a conversion to pay off (McQuarrie)
  • Tax drag on savings changes everything (Vanguard BETR)
  • Greatest benefit: Taxpayers who can convert in the 0% or 10% bracket during early retirement
  • Supersavers face unique challenges: Large Traditional balances create their own high-bracket problem via RMDs

💰 The Tax Arbitrage Opportunity:

Example Scenario:
• While Working: Traditional contributions at 32-37% marginal rate
• Early Retirement (ages 59-74): Convert at 0-12% marginal rate
• Net Benefit: 20-37% tax savings per dollar, plus decades of tax-free compounding

⏰ The Time Factor (McQuarrie):

Roth conversions work because of compounding. Given enough time, tax-free growth always overcomes an initial tax hit. The "Roman centurion" thought experiment: tax-free 4% growth eventually produces 100× the wealth of taxed 3.52% growth—it just takes time.

🔢 Why RMDs Break the "Indifference Theorem":

The theory that equal tax rates produce equal outcomes assumes lump-sum withdrawals. But RMDs force you to withdraw as a stream, which:

  • Reduces the compounding base annually
  • Triggers Social Security taxation and IRMAA surcharges
  • Removes flexibility—you must withdraw whether needed or not

Bottom Line: For high earners with substantial Traditional balances, both methods point to the same conclusion: strategic Roth conversions during low-income years can dramatically reduce lifetime taxes. The full calculator models your specific situation to quantify the benefit.

Calculator Methodology:

Full Lifecycle Analysis: Models every year through age 90, including RMDs, conversions, Social Security, IRMAA, and NIIT.

BETR Analysis: Calculates break-even rate based on how tax savings are invested (tax-efficient, tax-inefficient, cash, or Roth-equivalent).

Conflict Detection: Warns when BETR and full calculation disagree, explaining why (usually the supersaver problem).

Time Value of Money: All taxes compounded forward to age 90 to capture opportunity cost.

Inflation Adjustments: All brackets, deductions, and thresholds inflate annually.

Note on RMD Age: This calculator assumes an RMD age of 75, which applies to individuals born after December 31, 1959. If you were born earlier, your RMD age may be different:

  • Before July 1, 1949: RMD age 70½
  • July 1, 1949 to Dec. 31, 1950: RMD age 72
  • Jan. 1, 1951 to Dec. 31, 1959: RMD age 73
  • After Dec. 31, 1959: RMD age 75
